# C04 — Verification and NP

**Status:** Editorial draft, pending independent review.

## Question

Finding an answer and checking a proposed answer are different activities. A satisfying assignment for a Boolean formula can be checked by substituting its variable values, even when discovering the assignment is difficult.

## Formal definition

A decision language (L) is in (NP) if there exist a polynomial (p) and deterministic polynomial-time verifier (V) such that

[
xin L iff exists w, |w|le p(|x|) land V(x,w)=1.
]

The witness (w) must be polynomial in input length. Soundness means no witness is accepted on a no-instance; completeness means some witness is accepted on every yes-instance.

## Worked example

For (arphi=(alor b)land(
eg alor c)), the assignment (a=1,b=0,c=0) satisfies both clauses. Verification evaluates each clause and therefore takes polynomial time in the encoded formula's length. For an unsatisfiable formula, no assignment will pass.

A Hamiltonian-cycle certificate lists vertices in cyclic order; checking adjacency, length, and uniqueness is polynomial. For decision TSP, the verifier additionally adds tour weights and checks the threshold.

## Proven containment

**Theorem.** \(P\subseteq NP\).

**Proof.** If (D) decides (L) in polynomial time, choose verifier (V(x,w)=D(x)), ignoring its witness, and allow the empty string. Then the defining equivalence holds. (square)

Strict inclusion \(P\subsetneq NP\) is **not proved**: whether \(P=NP\) remains open. NP does not mean “non-polynomial.” It stands for nondeterministic polynomial time, an equivalent machine-based characterization whose accepting branches can be encoded as certificates.

## Boundary

An easy-to-check witness for a yes-answer is not automatically an easy way to find the witness or to certify a no-answer. Membership in NP is not NP-hardness and does not establish completeness.
