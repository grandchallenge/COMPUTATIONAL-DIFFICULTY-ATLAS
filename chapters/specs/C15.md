# C15 — Quantum Computation

Status: keystone specification v0.1

## Pedagogical objective

The reader should understand quantum computation as a different computational model with a specific complexity class, not as a universal mechanism for solving classically hard problems.

## Reader-facing question

Which computational tasks become efficiently solvable when algorithms may use quantum states, interference, and measurement?

## Core distinctions

- quantum speedup vs generic parallelism;
- BQP vs NP;
- bounded error vs exact computation;
- decision-class statement vs function-problem algorithm;
- polynomial speedup vs exponential/superpolynomial speedup.

## Anchor problems

- **Integer factorization:** central anchor through Shor's algorithm.
- **Unstructured search:** Grover as a contrasting quadratic speedup.
- **SAT:** used explicitly to reject the claim that known quantum algorithms place NP-complete problems in BQP.

## Formal payload

Introduce uniform polynomial-size quantum circuits and BQP at a conceptual/formal level.

State accurately:
- \(P \subseteq BQP\);
- factoring and discrete logarithm admit polynomial-time quantum algorithms via Shor-type methods;
- Grover gives quadratic query speedup for unstructured search;
- it is not known that \(NP \subseteq BQP\), nor that NP-complete problems are efficiently quantum-solvable in general.

QMA may be introduced as optional depth.

## Misconception obligations

M05, M06, M20.

## Figure obligations

- classical vs quantum resource-map overlay;
- Shor/factoring anchor trajectory;
- Grover quadratic-scaling plot;
- a conspicuous "not implied" relation between BQP and NP-complete.

## Epistemic-status requirements

No claim about BQP versus NP may be rendered as a proved separation or containment beyond what is known.

## Exercises / thought experiments

1. Compare \(N\) vs \(\sqrt N\) query scaling.
2. Explain why Shor does not imply an efficient quantum SAT solver.
3. Spot the defect in "quantum computers try all answers at once."
4. Distinguish a BQP decision statement from the function task of returning prime factors.

## Map update

The reader learns that changing the computational model changes the map, but does not collapse all other notions of hardness.

## Exit test

The reader can explain why factoring is the right example of a dramatic quantum algorithm and the wrong example for saying "quantum solves NP-complete problems."
