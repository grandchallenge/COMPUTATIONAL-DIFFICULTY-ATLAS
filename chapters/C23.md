# C23 — The Atlas: A Multidimensional Map of Computational Difficulty

**Status:** Editorial candidate; integrated figures and publication review pending.
**Prerequisites:** C01–C22.

## Question: what does it mean to say one problem is harder than another?

There is no universal altitude called difficulty. A problem can be easy to verify, hard for a specified proof system, and amenable to a structural shortcut. The Atlas supplies coordinates, not one ladder.

## Section 1 — Containment

For standard uniform decision classes,

$$
P\subseteq NP\subseteq PH\subseteq PSPACE\subseteq EXPTIME.
$$

The time hierarchy gives $P\ne EXPTIME$ but does not identify which adjacent inclusion is strict. P = NP remains open. P is contained in BQP, which is contained in PSPACE; neither general NP/BQP containment is known. RE contains decidable languages and undecidable recognizable ones. Inclusion is not an instance difficulty ranking.

## Section 2 — Reduction relations

An arrow from $A$ to $B$ means a solver for $B$ can be used to solve $A$ in the specified sense. Distinguish polynomial many-one, logspace, parameterized, gap-preserving, distribution-dominating, computable many-one and oracle reductions. A theorem for one edge type cannot silently be used for another. Completeness is relative to both class and reduction.

## Section 3 — Resource coordinates

Time counts operations; space counts memory; parallel work and depth count operations and dependencies. Query models charge oracle access. Quantum computation specifies allowed gates and uniformity. Interactive proofs constrain verifier resources and prover communication. Approximation adds objective quality, parameterization adds a separate size measure, and average-case analysis adds a distribution. Improvement on one coordinate may worsen another.

## Section 4 — Anchors revisited

- SAT: NP-completeness, decision/search equivalence, variable-count parameter and model-specific proof bounds.
- TSP: decision, optimization and approximation differ; metric and general formulations require different assumptions.
- QBF: standard alternating formula problem is PSPACE-complete.
- Factoring: a function task with quantum algorithms, not a known NP-complete decision language.
- HALT: recognizable but undecidable; RE-complete under computable many-one reductions.

## Section 5 — Research case study: the transfer boundary

GCL PNP-ABOVE WP37 proves large semantic refutation width for a specified bounded-support binary inference system on explicit parity constraints. Yet the varying-charge family is polynomial-time decidable by aggregate parity. Width alone is not an unrestricted time lower bound.

WP38A/B formalize, in a pinned native computation model and under the explicit verifier boundedness premise, equivalence of polynomial SAT decision, arbitrary witness construction, canonical witness construction and restricted valid compressed promise solving. Standard SAT self-reducibility underlies this result; the project-specific work is exact native compilation and scope accounting. An unrestricted witness-construction lower bound would suffice within that model; it remains unproved. Standard Turing-machine concordance, P versus NP resolution and independent certification do not follow. MIP* = RE concerns a distinct verification model and does not close this gap.

## Section 6 — How to read a claim

Identify encoding and promise, output type, machine and uniformity, charged resources, quantified algorithm scope, reduction type and cost, and proved versus conditional status. Ask whether the transfer itself is proved. Wide resolution clauses alone do not establish that SAT is slow for all algorithms.
